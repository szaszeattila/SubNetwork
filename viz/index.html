About

What this is

A map of what drugs developed for neurological and psychiatric conditions, and a few reference molecules such as theobromine, act on: the proteins, how each molecule acts there (agonist, antagonist, inhibitor, …), and how much published evidence stands behind each claim. It is drawn from public research databases and is exploratory and educational.

Not medical advice

Nothing here is medical, dosing or safety guidance. Do not start, stop, switch or combine any medicine because of this page; ask a doctor or pharmacist.

This page does not cover interactions between drugs. Enzymes that break drugs down, such as CYP2D6, are drawn as targets like any receptor: two molecules sharing one were both measured there, which says nothing reliable about taking them together, and a missing line does not mean they are safe together.

What "similar" means here

Closest target profiles ranks molecules by the targets they share, weighted by how well each claim is evidenced, discounted by how common the target is, and divided by how much each molecule does. That is a much weaker claim than "these two drugs act alike". It knows nothing about dose, exposure, or whether a molecule reaches the brain at all, and two molecules close here are not substitutes for each other. It is a place to start looking, not a verdict.

"Approved" and trial statuses are as ChEMBL and ClinicalTrials.gov recorded them when this page was built, except where ChEMBL's only evidence of an approval is US labels that FDA lists as marketed without approval: those read "Marketed unapproved", or the phase of the trials ChEMBL cites. They may be out of date, and they say nothing about whether a drug is right for anyone.

Known limitations

  • The weights in the similarity score are set by argument, not fitted. Graded against the WHO's ATC drug classes, a molecule's top match shares its class well under half the time.
  • How strongly a molecule binds is not weighed: 7 nM and 200 µM count alike.
  • There is no brain-expression data; the brain focus comes only from which molecules were chosen.
  • Source records can be wrong. One known ChEMBL error is corrected for: ChEMBL files alfuzosin as an alpha-1 agonist, where the label it cites calls it an antagonist, so the page withholds that direction and says so where it would have appeared. No other has been found, which is not the same as there being none.

Report an error

If something here is wrong, please open an issue on GitHub, naming the molecule, the target and the source that says otherwise. The code and its documentation are on GitHub too, and contributions are welcome.
